## What Is Abacus AI Bot?

First, a clarification that will save you confusion. "Abacus AI Bot" isn't an official product name. Abacus.AI is the company, and the "bot" people talk about is usually one of these:

- **ChatLLM**, the chat assistant that gives you access to many AI models in one place.
- **DeepAgent** (now also described as the Abacus AI Agent in some places), the agent that takes a goal and works through the steps for you.
- **Custom chatbots and AI Workflows**, which are bots you build for a specific job, like answering customer questions from your own documents.

Abacus.AI is a San Francisco company founded in 2019 by Bindu Reddy and Arvind Sundararajan. It started as RealityEngines.AI and focused on machine learning for enterprises. Over time it moved into the "AI super assistant" space: one product that covers chat, coding, research, image and video generation, and automation.

Think of it as a small team of digital helpers. One talks with you. Another goes away and does the work.

### How an agent differs from a normal chatbot

A normal chatbot answers questions. An agent completes tasks. That's the difference that matters for your time.

With a chatbot you ask, "Write me a report outline," and you get text back. With an agent you say, "Research my top five local competitors, put their services and prices in a table, build a simple dashboard, and send me an update every Monday." The agent plans the steps, uses tools, and returns a finished result.

## What does Abacus AI cost?

Pricing is the first thing most owners ask about, so here's a plain answer.

- **ChatLLM Teams** is widely reported at about **$10 per user per month**, and several reviewers mention a free first month. DeepAgent access is bundled into the platform.
- The platform uses a **credit-based system**. On the basic plan, reviewers report limits such as 3 agent conversations with a cap of 2,500 credits per conversation. A higher plan was later introduced with looser limits.
- **Enterprise** pricing is custom. One reviewer put it at around $5,000 per month for full API access, so treat that as a rough reference, not a quote.

Credits are the thing to watch. A simple task uses few. A big, multi-step build can use a lot. Start with one small project, check your usage after a week, and then decide whether to upgrade.

We haven't run a paid Abacus deployment for a client, so treat these figures as a starting point. Check the official pricing page before you commit.

## How to automate your workflow with Abacus AI (step by step)

The practical part. You don't need to be technical to follow it.

### Step 1: Pick one boring, repeating task

Don't start with "automate my whole business." Choose one task that:

- You do at least weekly
- Follows roughly the same steps every time
- Doesn't carry big legal or financial risk if it goes slightly wrong

Good first picks are a weekly sales summary, a competitor check, a draft reply to common customer questions, or a monthly content plan.

### Step 2: Write the task like a brief, not a wish

The quality of what you get back depends on the quality of what you ask for. A good brief has four parts:

1. **Goal:** What should exist at the end?
2. **Inputs:** What information or files should it use?
3. **Format:** A table, a document, a dashboard, an email draft?
4. **Schedule:** Once, or on repeat?

**Weak prompt:** "Help me with my marketing."

**Strong prompt:** "Every Monday, review my last 7 days of Google Business Profile reviews, group them into praise and complaints, write a short summary for the owner, and draft polite replies to any review under 4 stars. Give me the result as a one-page document."

If you want to get better at this skill, our guide on [writing an effective system prompt](/p/what-is-system-prompt-how-to-write-effective/) covers the same thinking in more depth.

### Step 3: Choose a chatbot or a workflow

Abacus.AI offers a rule of thumb in its own documentation:

- If the job is mostly **conversation** (FAQs, recommendations, answering from your documents), build a **custom chatbot**.
- If the job needs **multiple steps, tools, or automation** (processing documents, updating a CRM, sending reports), build an **AI Workflow**. See [chatbots vs AI workflows](https://abacus.ai/help/chatllm-ai-super-assistant/faqs/chatbots-vs-workflows) for more details.

### Step 4: Connect your tools and data

Agents get more useful when they can reach the places your work lives: spreadsheets, documents, email, a CRM, or your website. Connect only what the task needs. Less access means less risk.

### Step 5: Test with real but low-risk examples

Run the task three or four times and read every output closely. Reviewers who like the platform still mention that DeepAgent can misread an edge case, so a human check at the start is good practice.

### Step 6: Schedule it

Once the output is reliable, set it to run on its own. Abacus workflows support cron-style timing and presets such as a daily 9 AM run, so you can say "every weekday morning" or "first of the month" without learning the syntax.

### Step 7: Review monthly and improve

Every month, look at what the automation saved you and what it got wrong. Update the instructions. Small edits add up over time.

## Before you automate, fix the inputs

Automation can't fix a messy process. It makes the mess move faster. Before you hand a task to an agent, make sure the source data is current, the output has a clear owner, and someone can spot a bad result.

Three checks save most projects:

- **Is the input clean?** If your prices, hours, or service list disagree across your site and your listings, the agent will pick one and sound confident about it.
- **Is the output easy to review?** A short table or a draft email beats a 20-page document nobody reads.
- **Can you undo it?** Keep a human approval step for anything that sends email, changes a record, or touches money.

This is also where a clear website and complete business listings help. An agent that drafts replies still needs accurate service details, prices, and hours to work from. When those live in five places and disagree, the automation will sound confident and be wrong.
